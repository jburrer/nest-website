Our History

  Since its accidental beginning in the weeks leading up to Halloween of 2023, we at The Nest have been hosting DIY live music nearly every weekend. We’ve provided a consistent third space with the mission of fostering intentional community amongst our audience of Lafayette-area locals and Purdue students, simultaneously providing a platform for both our beloved local artists and underground touring acts from across the US. DIY music is unmatched in its ability to bring young people together and help meaningfully connect; we see it as an antidote to the widespread alienation pervading our culture. We keep our values front and center: the community we foster must be feminist, anti-racist, and queer-oriented. This is no easy feat and places our culture in stark contrast with that of the mainstream, leaving us to grapple with questions of accountability, democracy, restorative justice, and more.

  Over the 3 years we’ve been operating, we’ve evolved into a self-organized, 30-person team comprised mostly of Purdue students all between the ages of 18 and 23. We’re well on our way toward operating a nonprofit, worker-cooperative, brick-and-mortar music venue funded by small-dollar donations raised at our shows, but it's been a long and rocky road to get here. We’ve been shut down by landlords and our city government multiple times and have frequently dealt with police surveillance and harassment, but all of that has ultimately strengthened our commitment to the local community and reinforced our mission to open our own brick-and-mortar music venue that’s a permanent refuge from these threats and gives our community a place to flourish.

Some photos from shows throughout the Nest's first 3 years.

  The name “The Nest” comes from our founder Johnny Burrer’s midwest emo band “whirly birds”, which he started his freshman year at Purdue. At the beginning of his sophomore year, he moved into a house about a block away from Purdue campus which soon became our birthplace, 207 W Stadium Avenue. Its dingy and unassuming basement became the band’s first consistent place to practice, which they jokingly nicknamed “The Nest”. whirly birds’ bassist Rio Wagabaza lived in another house directly across the alley which featured a gravel lot out back, and in late August of 2023 he began allowing local bands to organize shows there. The outdoor nature of the space meant that sound carried throughout the whole neighborhood, and police arrived and shut down one of these early events before the 2nd band had finished playing. In the heat of the moment, the decision was made to rapidly clear out 207 W Stadium’s basement and lug all the equipment across the alley to let the show continue. Despite the chaos, the night ended up being a blast and accidentally giving birth to the Nest. Soon after, Johnny, Rio, and members of several other bands began planning to host a sort of DIY “festival” where Johnny’s basement and Rio’s lot acted as 2 stages and 16 bands could play over the course of 2 days, with the crowd moving back and forth between the stages across the alley. When the day finally came, cops shut down the first set almost immediately, leaving us no choice other than to figure out how to host all 16 bands at 207 W Stadium. It once again made for an intense and chaotic weekend but ultimately proved to be a huge success! That Halloweekend marked the official beginning of the venue under the Nest name.
